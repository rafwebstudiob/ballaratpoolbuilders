VIC pool fencing and approvals in Ballarat

In short: A new pool or spa in Victoria needs a building permit from a registered building surveyor, a barrier that meets AS 1926, registration with council, and a certificate of barrier compliance.

A summary of official rules, with links to the source pages. It is not legal advice.

Last updated: 24 November 2026

This page summarises official Victorian rules so you know what to expect. It is not legal advice and doesn't replace a registered building surveyor. Follow the links and ask the builder or surveyor if you're unsure.

The short answer

The Building and Plumbing Commission (BPC, which replaced the Victorian Building Authority) says you need a building permit before installing a pool, spa or safety barrier unless the work is exempt. The owner appoints a registered building surveyor (private or council) to issue it. You may also need a planning permit, so check with the council.

Source: BPC, Installing a new pool or spa.

What the permit application needs

The application must include detailed drawings and specifications for the pool, spa, safety barrier and water recirculation system, showing how the design complies with the National Construction Code and AS 1926 Parts 1, 2 and 3. Work must start within 12 months of the permit being issued, and once started must generally be finished within six months.

Local council: Ballarat

Your local council is City of Ballarat. Check the council's planning information for your address (zoning, overlays, bush fire, flood or heritage mapping), as these can change what is allowed for a pool. Your builder or certifier normally does this check as part of the quote.

Many inner Ballarat streets are covered by heritage overlays in the planning scheme, which can affect what you can change on the outside of a house and, sometimes, new structures in the backyard. Check the overlay mapping, or ask the builder, before you settle on a design.

Registration and the barrier certificate

Once the pool or spa is built, you must register it with council and give council a certificate of barrier compliance from a qualified inspector. For pools and spas built after 1 November 2020, the certificate goes in with the registration, within 30 days of the occupancy permit or certificate of final inspection. The barrier must then be inspected and recertified every four years. Fines apply for failing to register.

Sources: BPC, Pool and spa registration requirements; BPC, Inspections and compliance.

Builders, contracts and insurance

The BPC says to use a builder registered in the right category. If you choose to be an owner-builder, you need a certificate of consent from the BPC for work over $16,000.

In Victoria, domestic building work over $10,000 must be done by a registered builder under a written major domestic building contract. For work over $16,000, the builder must give you a domestic building insurance policy and certificate of currency before taking any payment. The deposit can be no more than 10% if the contract price is under $20,000, or 5% if it is $20,000 or more. Victoria's building regulator is now the Building and Plumbing Commission (BPC), which replaced the VBA, and you can check a builder's registration with it.

Sources: Consumer Affairs Victoria, What work requires a building contract; Consumer Affairs Victoria, Deposits and payments; Consumer Affairs Victoria, Domestic building insurance.

Official pages checked 23 November 2026. Rules can change; check the linked sources or a building surveyor before relying on this summary.
